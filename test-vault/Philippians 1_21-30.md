# Philippians 1:21–30

Paul's dilemma and the charge that follows it. The passage runs as a cascade of inferences: a premise
about living and dying, the dilemma it creates, the resolution, and then the imperative that carries
the weight.

## Why each bracket

| Propositions | Relationship | Signal |
|---|---|---|
| 21-26, 27-30 | Inference | The imperative `πολιτεύεσθε` carries the passage. `Μόνον` pivots from Paul's circumstances to their conduct. |
| 21-24, 25-26 | Inference | `τοῦτο` in verse 25 points back to verse 24: having been persuaded that remaining is necessary, Paul knows he will remain. |
| 21-22b, 22c-24 | Inference | The dilemma follows from both outcomes being good. |
| 21a, 21b | Alternative | Living and dying are the two possibilities, and `ἐκ τῶν δύο` in verse 23 takes its antecedent from here. |
| 21, 22a-b | Idea-Explanation | Verse 22 spells out what "to live is Christ" amounts to in practice. |
| 22a, 22b | Conditional | `εἰ`. |
| 22c, 23a | Series | Two statements of the same dilemma. |
| 22c-23a, 23b-24 | Idea-Explanation | The two options named. |
| 23b-c, 24 | Alternative | `δέ`. Depart, or remain. |
| 23b, 23c | Ground | `πολλῷ γὰρ μᾶλλον κρεῖσσον`. NA28 brackets the `γάρ`; the CSB drops it, which is why the arm is not obvious from the English. |
| 25a, 25b-26 | Inference | The causal participle `πεποιθώς` precedes what it supports, which is what separates Inference from Ground in this method. |
| 25b, 25c | Action-Purpose | `εἰς` + accusative. |
| 25c, 26 | Action-Purpose | `ἵνα`, a further purpose beyond the first. |
| 27a, 27b-30 | Action-Purpose | `ἵνα`. |
| 27b, 27c-30 | Idea-Explanation | `ὅτι` gives the content of what Paul would hear. |
| 27c, 28a | Series | Standing firm and contending, and not being frightened. |
| 27c-28a, 28b-30 | Idea-Explanation | `ἥτις` names what their fearlessness signifies. |
| 28b, 28c | Series | The sign, then its source. |
| 28b-c, 29-30 | Ground | `ὅτι`. The sign is from God because God granted both the believing and the suffering. |
| 29, 30 | Ground | The participle `ἔχοντες`, which the CSB renders "since". |
| 29a, 29b-c | Idea-Explanation | The content of what was granted. |
| 29b, 29c | Negative-Positive | `οὐ μόνον ... ἀλλὰ καί`. |

## Notes

- **The spine is four inferences.** Premise, dilemma, resolution, charge. Each step is drawn from the
  one before it, and only the last carries a star at the top level.
- **Verse 21 as an Alternative is the one label doing interpretive work.** The conjunction is `καί`,
  which argues for Series. The reason for Alternative is `συνέχομαι δὲ ἐκ τῶν δύο` in verse 23: "the
  two" has no antecedent unless verse 21 has already set living and dying side by side as options.
  Change `A` to `S` to see the conservative reading.
- **The tie at verse 27 is the loosest joint.** `Μόνον` marks a turn rather than a conclusion, and many
  would start a new unit here. Read as coordinate, the top becomes `S` or `P` between 21-26 and 27-30,
  with no main point at all.
- **Verse 30 could be Idea-Explanation rather than Ground**, identifying what the granted suffering
  actually is instead of giving a reason for it. The participle `ἔχοντες` is open either way; the CSB's
  "since" is what settles it here.
