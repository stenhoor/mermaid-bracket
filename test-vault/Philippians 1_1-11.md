# Philippians 1:1–11

Greeting, then two mirrored movements. Verses 3–7 carry the idea and verses 8–11 the purpose, and
the second is the main point the first supports. Each movement runs God, then prayer, then the day of
Christ.

## Why each bracket

| Propositions | Relationship | Signal |
|---|---|---|
| 1, 2 | Series | Two halves of one greeting. |
| 1-2, 3-11 | Series | Greeting, then the body. |
| 3-7, 8-11 | Inference | Support precedes its statement. The idea of 3-7 stands behind the purpose of 8-11, which carries the weight of the passage. |
| 3-5, 6-7 | Series | The two halves of the first movement. |
| 8, 9-11 | Series | The two halves of the second movement, mirroring the first: God, then prayer. |
| 3, 4 | Progression | Thanks, then prayer with joy. `ποιούμενος` is a participle, so Action-Manner is the alternative. |
| 3-4, 5 | Ground | `ἐπί` + dative, "because of your partnership". |
| 6, 7 | Comparison | `καθώς`. |
| 6a, 6b-c | Idea-Explanation | `ὅτι` states the content of `αὐτὸ τοῦτο`. |
| 6b, 6c | Progression | `ἐναρξάμενος` then `ἐπιτελέσει`: begun, then carried to completion at the day of Christ. |
| 8a, 8b | Comparison | `ὡς`. As a content clause after `μάρτυς`, Idea-Explanation is the alternative. |
| 9a, 9b-11 | Idea-Explanation | `ἵνα` after `τοῦτο προσεύχομαι` states what the prayer is. |
| 9b, 10-11 | Action-Purpose | `εἰς τό` + infinitive. |
| 10a, 10b, 11 | Progression | Approve what is superior, then be pure and blameless at the day of Christ, then bear fruit to God's glory. |

## Notes on the splits

- **6b is a subject phrase in English.** `ὁ ἐναρξάμενος` is an articular participle doing subject duty
  for `ἐπιτελέσει`, so the CSB line "that he who started a good work in you" has no predicate of its
  own. The Greek participle carries the verbal force, which is what makes the progression visible; the
  English is left as the CSB writes it rather than reworded.
- **Verse 8 opens the second movement.** The `γάρ` looks backward, and the phrase "all of you"
  (`πάντων ὑμῶν`, `πάντας ὑμᾶς`) runs through verses 4, 7 and 8 and appears nowhere in 9-11, so the
  verse is lexically anchored to what precedes it. It is placed with 9-11 because of what it does
  rather than what it echoes: God as witness, then the prayer, matching God thanked, then the prayer,
  in the first movement. Bilateral is the label for a proposition supporting in both directions, and
  it fits if Paul's longing is the warrant for the petition rather than merely preceding it. The
  renderer draws Bilateral as an ordinary bracket, so that reading would live in the source only.
- **The word for day occurs three times and only three times**: `ἀπὸ τῆς πρώτης ἡμέρας` in verse 5,
  `ἄχρι ἡμέρας Χριστοῦ Ἰησοῦ` closing the first movement, `εἰς ἡμέραν Χριστοῦ` closing the second.
- **Verse 7 is left whole.** It contains `διὰ τό` + infinitive and the participle `ὄντας`, two reasons
  that could be split out under a `G` if the internal argument matters more than the comparison.
- **The `τε ... καί` pair in verse 7** binds "in my chains" to "in the defense and confirmation of the
  gospel"; both together locate the single participle `ὄντας`. They are coordinate with each other, not
  separate beats.
